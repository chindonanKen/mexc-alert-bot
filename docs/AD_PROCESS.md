# AD trading rules

This book is process only. No live play belongs here. Each play lives in its own file.

If a new sentence conflicts with one below, say so and wait. Do not hang a broken pack.

## Chart
- This chart’s repeats only. No repeat = skip.
- AD is the length that keeps bouncing in this range and TF. One or two hits then many fails is out.
- Write top then bottom. Official named bar, tooltip, or Kenneth typed it. Manila time.
- New range can print a new length. Do not force an old dump onto a later range.
- A bounce that is significant vs the smaller rallies on this chart is how we find the AD top. That is measure, not an exit.

## Path
- Reds and volume from this chart, not a general template.
- Chosen TF is the AD play (example: 4h). First or second red on that TF: sit out on a normal dump.
- Override: last is at the AD, and a faster TF on this dump shows a bottom hint — reds plus volume coming in at the line. The faster TF is not fixed. Kenneth calls the override when he sees it. We log every play: chosen TF reds, faster-TF reds, volume at the AD, take or sit, then what the bounce did. That log is the weigh. Do not hang 15m ≥ 3 as law. Not hung on the Machine. Kenneth ❤️ then 👍 locked this.
- No volume = size down, not skip.
- Around the AD is enough for the first 10% of the play only when the hit is not clean (grind / no volume). A convincing tag (volume at the line, last on the AD) takes the first 10% at that hit. Kenneth 👍 locked this.

## Size
- Write in percent of the account and of the play. Do not lock dollar amounts in this document.
- One play = 50% of the account. Max two plays.
- First 10% of the play at the AD when the hit is clean, or around the AD when it is not.
- Layer toward the AD bottom. Biggest of the first 3–5 at or just under the AD.
- Keep 50% of the play for panic beyond the AD, unless Kenneth recuts that play. Breaking the AD is usually add, not flatten. That half is where this process earns.

## Fail
- If last breaks the AD, add with the panic half (50% of the play), unless this play was recut. That is the earn, not the exit.
- A fail is only: (1) we are in, price grinds lower for a long time, and this chart’s history says a real AD bounce does not look like this; or (2) we played a short AD and this chart already had a longer copy that was the real one — the short one is a liability, stop adding that short copy.
- A timer is not a fail. It is risk. A fast dump from the top to the AD usually bounces fast. A slow grind down to the AD usually bounces slow. Prefer the fast dumps. Do not close a slow one because a clock ran out. Allow more time on a slow dump. That extra time is risk, not a fail.
- Last under the AD while adding under is not a fail by itself. That print is the add zone or the panic half.
- Flatten on real bad news. Not on a break. Not on a clock.

## Exit
- Default sell pack: 5 layers, exponential. Biggest sells on the 3rd and 4th layer.
- Exits are TF dependent. Exit layers sit toward the typical bounce on the chosen TF: how far a finished AD bounce on that TF usually runs, from official bars. If this TF has no repeating bounce, do not invent one. The Machine does not guess a target. It only uses the bounce habit Kenneth recut.
- If the drop went past the AD and it was not board-wide panic: defensive. Do not expect a normal bounce. Lower the exit layers compared to the original plan, scaled to the new bottom. Do not put the sells at the bottom.
- Board-wide panic (the best tape): do not sell the first weak bounce. Give it time. If price is under a big base, sell up into that base.
- Sideways too long is not a clock. Reed writes this TF’s bounce habit: how many candles after an AD tag a real bounce usually starts. If last is at the AD, those candles have passed, and there is no bounce like this chart usually prints, that is sideways too long. Then consider an exit. If this TF has no repeating start time, do not use this line. The Machine does not flatten on it until Kenneth recuts that chart’s number. Kenneth 👍 locked this.
- Isolated bad news: high caution. Broad panic: usually stay.
- Never fight a clear failed AD. Reassess. Weigh risk from this chart’s history. Send the chart agent to walk the chart for what we missed. Do not flatten from a clock.
